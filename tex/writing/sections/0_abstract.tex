\section*{Abstract}

\begin{center}
    I estimate the productivity spillover occuring between colocated firms in the United Kingdom.
    The productivity spillover, a spatial externality, underpins well-established economic phenomena
    around the development of urban clusters in the New Economic Geography literature,
    and is measured and heavily discussed in the evidence on the economic effects of urban density
    measured in the spatial econometrics literature, and underpinning regional growth policy.
    \par
    I apply recently developed econometric techniques for valid identification
    when estimating spillover effects in a spatial group and network setting
    to measures of UK firm's Total Factor Productivity (TFP).
    The key to valid identification rests in internal instruments from spatial \itx{exogenous} effects
    in a static model, and from deep lags in a dynamic model:
    this overcomes \cite{Manski1993}'s `Reflection Problem' and the endogeneity of peer effects.
    It requires big data calculations of firm interactions,
    which I use modern data techniques to compute efficiently,
    preserving the advantages of Fame's high-observation balance sheet data
    for both its panel structure and its resulting high-significant estimates.
    \par
    Across my instrumented specifications,
    I evaluate a spatial spillover of firm peers to any given target firm
    of an elasticity of \todo{0.2}, a significant proportion of GVA.
    These results imply that regional economic policy can have serious multiplier effects:
    more policy emphasis should be placed on raising the Total Factor Productivity
    of regional firms as its effects will spillover to its peers.

\end{center}
