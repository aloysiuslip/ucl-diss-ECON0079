\section{Dynamic models}
\label{s:models-dynamic}

The previous section's static model requires a factor exogeneity assumption
outlined in equation \eqref{eq:static-identification-factor-exogeneity}
for all firms, including the own-firm effect of the production function.
In this section, I use a dynamic model to relax this assumption.
I also introduce a vector of temporal regressors:
an autoregressor of own-firm GVA as a component of its TFP,
and allowing for the past peer TFP components I analysed
in the static model to influence current-period TFP
in a lagged fashion.
The structure of this specification now resembles a vector panel,
which I will denote with VAR(p,q)\footnote{noting that $q$-lags refers
to those on the peer spillover effect, rather than those of a moving average component
as is traditional of a VARMA(p,q) specification}.
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:dyn-main-reduced}
    z_{i,t} &= \alpha_i + \gamma_t + \beta_0 + \sum_{k=1}^p\phi_k y_{i,t-k}
        + \sum_{k=0}^q\mathbf z_{t-k}^{spill}\boldsymbol\theta_k + \varepsilon_{i,t}
    \\
    \label{eq:dyn-main-peer-effects}
    y_{i,t} &= \alpha_i + \gamma_t + \beta_0 + \beta_1 k_{i,t} + \beta_2 l_{i,t} + \varepsilon_{i,t}
                + \sum_{k=1}^p\phi_k y_{i,t-k}
                \\ \notag &\qquad 
                + \sum_{k=0}^q \left[
                  \delta_k \sum_{j\neq i} w_{ij}y_{j,t}
                + (\xi_k-\delta_k\beta_1) \cdot \sum_{j\neq i} w_{ij}k_{j,t}
                + (\zeta_k-\delta_k\beta_2) \cdot \sum_{j\neq i} w_{ij}l_{j,t}
                \right]
\end{align}
%
For discussion of the theory of the model, I simplify to display a VAR(1, 0) for now.
\begin{align}
    y_{i,t} &= \alpha_i + \gamma_t + \beta_0 + \beta_1 k_{i,t} + \beta_2 l_{i,t} + \varepsilon_{i,t}
                + \phi_1 y_{i,t-1}
                \\ \notag &\qquad 
                + \delta_0 \sum_{j\neq i} w_{ij}y_{j,t}
                + (\xi_0-\delta_0\beta_1) \cdot \sum_{j\neq i} w_{ij}k_{j,t}
                + (\zeta_0-\delta_0\beta_2) \cdot \sum_{j\neq i} w_{ij}l_{j,t}
\end{align}

There are significant advantages to introducing the $q$-lags of the spatial spillover.
This specification with both spatial and temporal lags accounts for the possibility
that the spillover effect from firm $j$'s production factor
has a lag to impact $i$. Without including the $q$ lags within $z_{i,t}$,
if $\mathbf z_{t-k}^\text{spill}$ is correlated with $\mathbf z_{t}$,
then estimating $\delta_0$ in a static model will have omitted variable bias (OVB)
of the lagged effect from the spillover attributed to the current period.
\par

Adding the autoregressor of \itx{gva1} to the specification is highly desirable
to a justified decomposition of TFP when including peer effects in the specification.
The panel setup with firm fixed effects already accounts for own-firm invariance,
and we have shown in the results already given that they account for a significant amount of the variation.
In the peer effects setting, the autoregressive lags help control for reverse causality.
If $y_{i,t-1}$ is causal for $z_{jt}$,
or feasibly $z_{j,t-1}$ and $\mathbb E[z_{j,t-1}, z_{j,t}]\neq0$, then the presence of
$y_{i,t-k}$ as a control helps eliminate that bias.
Similarly, theory offers us no clear view on where there could be simultaneity,
where variation in $\Delta y_{i,t}$ is driven causally by $\Delta y_{i,t-1}$
and that change in $\Delta y_{i,t}$ in turn drives $\Delta z_{j,t}$.
The autoregressive regressor helps control for that.
Additionally, the results below suggest the autoregressive parameter coefficient is low,
with a log elasticity of 0.005 (significant at the 1\% level) in all specifications,
suggesting any concerns about reverse causality or simultaneity driven by an AR process
are likely minimal.
The use of this dynamic regressor improves the plausible exogeneity of $z_{i,j,t}$.

\subsection{Dynamic panel identification}

The estimator given in \cite{Arellano1991} takes the regression equation
as given above and allows for endogeneity.
Internal instruments of all the endogenous regressors are found,
meaning their lagged first differences.
Identification is improved in the System GMM approach given in \cite{Blundell1998}
by also including the first logged, not differenced, lag of the endogenous regressors
which meets the usual instrument condiitons, as an instrument.
It is this System GMM approach that I follow here,
so I find the first logged, not differenced, lag of regressor in $x_{it}$
and its corresponding set of first difference lagged instruments,
which in practice just start from $\Delta x_{i,\tau-j}$ for any $j$
if $x_{i,\tau}$ is a valid instrument.
\par
I commence finding these valid instruments by assuming the structural specification (\ref{eq:static-main-gva})
exhibits no autocorrelation in its error term $\varepsilon_{i,t}$,
though I now allow for moderate cross-sectional correlation across firms.
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:dyn-no-ac}
    \mathbb E[\varepsilon_{i,t}\varepsilon_{i,t-1}] &= 0       ,\qquad \forall t,t-1
    \\
    \label{eq:dyn-contemperaneous-endogeneity}
    \mathbb E[x_{i,t}\varepsilon_{i,t}] &\neq 0              ,\qquad \exists i
    \\
    \label{eq:dyn-xs-corr-errors}
    \mathbb E[\varepsilon_{i,t}\varepsilon_{j,t}] &\neq 0   ,\qquad \exists i,j
    \\
    \label{eq:dyn-error-wn}
    \mathbb E[\varepsilon_t]    =0,
    \mathbb E[\varepsilon_t^2]  =\sigma_t^2,
    \mathbb E[\varepsilon_t|\Omega_{t-1}] &=0                  ,\qquad \forall i
    \qquad\text{(w.n.)}
    \\ \notag
    x_{i,t} &=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        k_{i,t} &   l_{i,t} &   \sum_{j\neq i} w_{ij,t}z_{j,t}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \\ \notag
    \Delta x_{i,t} &=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \Delta k_{i,t} &   \Delta l_{i,t} &   \Delta \sum_{j\neq i} w_{ij,t}z_{j,t}
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{align}

Taking each regressor in turn, I relax the exogeneity assumption
of the standard factors in the reduced-form equation, $\Delta k_{i,t}$ and $\Delta l_{i,t}$.
I assume a new optimal firm decision on capital based on information,
denoted by $\Omega_t$ which includes $\varepsilon_{i,t}$ for its respective $t$.
This approach allows the discovery of valid instruments which are exogenous to $\varepsilon_{i,t}$
and $\Delta\varepsilon_{i,t}$.
I justify an exclusion restriction for $\Delta k_{i,t-j}\ \forall j>1$, as it is predetermined,
and $\Delta l_{i,t-k}, k>2$, as it is flexible.
\begin{align}
    \Delta y_{i,t} &= \Delta \gamma_t
                + \beta_1\Delta k_{i,t}
                + \beta_2\Delta l_{i,t}
                + \delta_0\sum_{j\neq i} w_{ij}\Delta z_{j,t}
                + \Delta\varepsilon_{i,t}
    \\
    \label{eq:dyn-k-pim}
    K_{i,t} &= I_{i,t} + dK_{i,t-1}
    \\
    \label{eq:dyn-inv-information}
    I_{i,t} &= \mathbb{E}[I_{i,t}^* \mid \Omega_{t-1}]
    \\
    \label{eq:dyn-l-information}
    L_{i,t} &= \mathbb{E}[L_{i,t}^* \mid \Omega_t] 
    \\ \notag
    \varepsilon_{i,t} &\in \Omega_t
    \\ \notag
    &\implies K_{i,t} \propto \epsilon_{t-1}
    \\ \notag
    &\implies L_{i,t} \propto \epsilon_{t}
    \\ \notag
    \Delta \varepsilon_{i,t} &= \varepsilon_{i,t} - \varepsilon_{i,t-1}
    \\ \notag
    \Delta k_{i,t} &= k_{i,t} - k_{i,t-1}
        \propto \varepsilon_{i,t-1} \varepsilon_{i,t-2}
    \\ \notag
    &\implies \mathbb E[\Delta k_{i,t} \Delta \epsilon_t] \neq 0
    \\
    &\implies \mathbb E[\Delta k_{i,t-1} \Delta \epsilon_t] = 0, \mathbb E[k_{i,t-1} \varepsilon_t]=0
    \\ \notag
    \Delta l_{i,t} &= l_{i,t} - l_{i,t-1}
        \propto \varepsilon_{i,t}, \varepsilon_{i,t-1}
    \\
    &\implies \mathbb E[\Delta l_{i,t-2} \Delta \epsilon_t] = 0, \mathbb E[l_{i,t-2} \varepsilon_t]=0
\end{align}

\subsection{Autoregressive specification}

A dynamic specification grants the flexibility to add past realisations of variables
to the decomposition of TFP, $z_{i,t}$. This increases the number of regressors in reduced form,
improving the plausibility of its exogeneity with $\varepsilon_{i,t}$.
\begin{align}
    y_{i,t} &= \alpha_i + \gamma_t + \beta_0
                + \beta_1 k_{i,t}
                + \beta_2 l_{i,t}
                + \phi_1 y_{i,t-1}
                + \delta_0 \sum_{j\neq i} w_{ij}z_{j,t}
                + \varepsilon_{i,t}
\end{align}
The above equation is in structural form in the 1-stage regression,
but I can find an equivalent in the 2-stage regression with $z_{i,t}$ as the dependent variable.
\begin{align}
    z_{i,t} &= \alpha_i + \gamma_t + \beta_0
                + \phi_1 y_{i,t-1}
                + \delta_0 \sum_{j\neq i} w_{ij}z_{j,t}
                + \varepsilon_{i,t}
    \\
            &= \alpha_i + \gamma_t + \beta_0
                + \phi_1 \cdot (\beta_1 k_{i,t-1}, + \beta_2 l_{i,t-1} + z_{i,t-1})
                + \delta_0 \sum_{j\neq i} w_{ij}z_{j,t}
                + \varepsilon_{i,t}
\end{align}
For system GMM, I need to show that the reduced-form first difference regressors are exogenous.
\begin{align}
    \Delta y_{i,t} &= \Delta \gamma_t
                + \beta_1 \Delta k_{i,t}
                + \beta_2 \Delta l_{i,t}
                + \sum_{k=1}^p \phi_k \Delta y_{i,t-k}
                + \delta_0 \sum_{j\neq i} w_{ij,t} \Delta z_{j,t}
                + \Delta \varepsilon_{i,t}
    \\
    \Delta z_{i,t} &= \Delta\gamma_t
                + \phi_1 \cdot (\beta_1 \Delta k_{i,t-1}, + \beta_2 \Delta l_{i,t-1} + \Delta z_{i,t-1})
                + \delta_0 \sum_{j\neq i} w_{ij}\Delta z_{j,t}
                + \Delta \varepsilon_{i,t}
\end{align}

The first-order autoregressor, $\Delta ln(y_{it})$ is endogenous.
\begin{align}
    \Delta \varepsilon_{i,t} &= \varepsilon_{i,t} - \varepsilon_{i,t-1}
    \\ \notag
    \Delta y_{i,t-1} &= \qquad\dots\qquad + \Delta\varepsilon_{i,t-1}
    \\ \notag
    &= \qquad\dots\qquad + \varepsilon_{i,t-1} - \varepsilon_{i,t-2}
    \\ \notag
    &\implies \mathbb E[ \Delta y_{i,t-1}\Delta\varepsilon_{i,t}] \neq 0
    \\ \notag
    \Delta y_{i,t-2} &= \qquad\dots\qquad + \varepsilon_{i,t-2} - \varepsilon_{i,t-3}
    \\ \notag
    &\implies \mathbb E[ \Delta y_{i,t-1}\Delta\varepsilon_{i,t}] = 0
\end{align}
